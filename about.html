About us

A national organization, rooted in Afghan communities.

Our origin, the principles that guide us, and the humanitarian context that shapes every programme we design.

Introduction

Non-political. Non-sectarian. Locally led.

AHNO is a non-political, non-sectarian organization whose primary goal is humanitarian service delivery — assisting with social welfare, health and nutrition, access to quality education, agricultural livelihoods and irrigation, food security and safety, WASH, and shelter management. AHNO is registered under registration number 5763 with the Ministry of Economy of the Afghanistan Government.

AHNO is a non-profit national organization dedicated to delivering life-saving humanitarian assistance and early recovery support to vulnerable populations across Afghanistan, operating with a strong commitment to humanitarian principles, community participation, accountability, and cost-effective programming.

A complementary objective is to build capacity for locally directed community development, encouraging the establishment of social structures capable of identifying, evaluating, planning and implementing solutions to the concerns communities identify themselves.

AHNO was founded by a team of experienced Afghan professionals under the leadership of Dr. Jundullah Nabeel, with deep expertise in humanitarian response, development programming, monitoring and evaluation, and community-based interventions. The organization prioritizes locally driven solutions and works closely with communities, local authorities, and humanitarian partners to address urgent needs and promote resilience.

AHNO's strategy focuses on marginalized and economically deprived segments of society — with particular attention to the destitute, minorities, returnees, women and children — on an effective, participatory basis. Community mobilization and close coordination with line agencies at local, district and provincial level is integral to AHNO's approach before and during every project. AHNO also draws on its expertise in relationship-building with community elders: shuras remain an influential mechanism for humanitarian intervention and the identification of target areas.

Vision

Resilient, healthy, educated and economically independent Afghan communities living with dignity, self-reliance, and equal access to essential services.

Mission

To deliver timely, inclusive, and accountable humanitarian and early recovery assistance to vulnerable populations across Afghanistan through community-centered, evidence-based, and partnership-driven interventions.

What guides us

Core values.

Humanity & Neutrality

Community Participation

Empowering communities as active partners in identifying needs and shaping solutions.

Accountability & Transparency

Upholding high standards of integrity toward affected populations, donors, and partners.

Learning & Adaptation

Applying lessons learned to continuously improve programme quality and effectiveness.

What we're working toward

Strategic objectives.

  • Provide life-saving humanitarian assistance to populations affected by conflict, displacement, and natural disasters.
  • Improve access to quality health, nutrition, education, and WASH services.
  • Strengthen food security, livelihoods, agriculture, and irrigation systems.
  • Support women's empowerment and inclusive community development.
  • Enhance local resilience through capacity building and evidence-based programming.
  • Raise awareness and mobilize communities to establish and strengthen Community Based Organizations (CBOs), Community Development Councils (CDCs), and community activists — building a collaborative relationship between civil society and public institutions.
Strategic issues

The Afghanistan humanitarian context.

Four structural issues shape where and how AHNO works — and why urgency and community-rooted delivery matter.

01

Widespread poverty, food insecurity and livelihood collapse

Afghanistan continues to face one of the world's largest humanitarian crises, driven by economic decline, climate shocks, and long-term structural poverty. A significant portion of the population remains unable to meet basic food and livelihood needs.

In 2026, an estimated 21.9 million people require humanitarian assistance, with 17.4 million projected to face acute food insecurity, reflecting deep structural vulnerability across rural and urban areas.

The crisis is further driven by drought, economic instability, forced returns, earthquakes, and reduced humanitarian funding — collectively reducing household income and increasing reliance on aid.

02

Limited access to quality education and protection risks for children, girls and women

Access to safe, inclusive, and quality education remains severely constrained, particularly for girls and vulnerable children. Humanitarian needs are closely linked to protection risks, poverty, and limited access to basic services.

Restrictions on education and movement have resulted in large numbers of girls being out of school, while economic hardship increases child labour, early marriage, and negative coping strategies.

Women, children, and marginalized groups are disproportionately affected by humanitarian shocks and face increased barriers to services, livelihoods, and education opportunities.

03

Declining agricultural productivity and weak market systems

Agriculture remains the primary livelihood source for many households, yet productivity continues to decline due to climate shocks, water scarcity, and lack of access to inputs, finance, and markets.

Persistent drought and environmental degradation have caused widespread crop failures and reduced agricultural income, particularly in rural areas.

Climate change, water shortages, and poor infrastructure limit farmers' ability to store, process, and access national and international markets — increasing post-harvest losses and reducing resilience.

04

Recurrent natural disasters and climate-induced humanitarian needs

Afghanistan is highly vulnerable to climate-related disasters, including drought, floods, earthquakes, and extreme weather events, which directly affect agriculture, water resources, shelter, and livelihoods.

Humanitarian needs are increasingly driven by climate-induced shocks and recurrent disasters, which repeatedly destroy assets, reduce food production, and weaken community coping capacity.

Climate shocks have contributed to water crises, declining crop yields, displacement, and worsening food insecurity across the country.

Funding mobilization

AHNO mobilizes funding from institutional donors, humanitarian partners, and philanthropic organizations supporting humanitarian response in Afghanistan — prioritizing multi-sector programming in agriculture and food security, education, social protection services, and basic health support for the most vulnerable, crisis-affected populations.

AHNO also strengthens partnerships with charitable foundations, private donors, and humanitarian networks to expand resources dedicated to humanitarian assistance and early recovery — through coordinated resource mobilization and transparent fund management.
